# Elementary proof

For a positive integer n, the result is 1<(2+√2)n−⌊(1+√2)⌊√2n⌋⌋<3.

Put s=√2, m=⌊sn⌋ and t=sn−m. Irrationality and n≥1 give 0<t<1. Let a=⌊(1+s)m⌋ and k=m+2n−a, an integer.

Since s²=2, (1+s)m=m+2n−st. The defining floor inequalities give st≤k<st+1. From 0<st<2, integrality implies 1≤k≤2.

Finally (2+s)n−a=t+k, strictly between 1 and 3. `Research.BeattySqrtTwo.conjecture` proves exactly this conclusion.
